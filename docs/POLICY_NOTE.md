# Special Topic: Capacity Discipline Meets Decarbonization

## Executive view
China steel strategy increasingly requires a joint read of volume discipline and process transition. The 2025–2026 MIIT stable-growth plan and the industrial carbon-peaking implementation plan point toward capacity management, product upgrading, digital monitoring and lower-carbon process development. For a strategy team, policy should therefore enter the market model through supply growth, capex intensity, EAF share and product-mix assumptions rather than as a narrative-only overlay.

## Model implications
1. Capacity replacement and compliance pressure can slow effective supply growth.
2. Higher EAF share changes scrap exposure and may reduce sensitivity to imported ore/coking coal, though electricity and scrap availability matter.
3. Product-upgrading policy supports scenario differentiation by mix, not only by tonnes.
4. Carbon and energy-efficiency requirements can widen the cost curve between modern and lagging assets.

## Monitoring checklist
Policy implementation dates; capacity replacement notices; crude-steel output; EAF share; scrap availability; energy/carbon standards; export policy; high-end product demand.
